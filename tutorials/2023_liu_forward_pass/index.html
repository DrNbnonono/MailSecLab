1. Problem & amplification chain

The paper argues that forwarding uniquely amplifies the SPF / DKIM / DMARC protocol stack because it introduces a third party that legitimately rewrites envelopes on behalf of the sender. Three structural reasons forwarding is hard to secure:

  1. Three parties, not two. The forwarder is between sender and receiver; its behavior (mail it forwards vs. relays vs. discards) is opaque to both.
  2. Forwarding's purpose is intrinsic opposition to anti-spoofing. Forwarders try to make a message appear as if it came directly from the original sender; DMARC's alignment check actively punishes that.
  3. No standard implementation. Every provider rewrites MAIL FROM / From differently; "good" implementations collide with DMARC's strict alignment, so providers make pragmatic decisions the protocols do not anticipate.

2. Four forwarding mechanisms

Two running examples: Alice auto-forwards from alice@univ.edu to alice@gmail.com; Bob posts to a list list@univ.edu which redistributes to user@univ.edu.

  1. PMF — Plain Message Forwarding. Forwarder rewrites only RCPT TO; MAIL FROM and From remain the sender's. Forwarder is invisible to the MUA. Breaks SPF: sender's SPF allowlist rarely permits the forwarder's IP.
  2. MFEF — MAIL FROM Equals FROM. Outlook/Freemail custom. Forwarder copies From into MAIL FROM so both fields reference the original sender. Still typically breaks SPF.
  3. REM — Remailing (redistribution). Forwarder rewrites both RCPT TO and MAIL FROM; the new MAIL FROM lives on the forwarder's domain (e.g. alice-xyz@univ.edu). Passes SPF but breaks DMARC alignment (FROM is unchanged).
  4. REM + MOD — Remailing with Modification. Like REM but the From is rewritten to the forwarder/list address (e.g. list@univ.edu). Passes SPF and DMARC but the forwarder becomes the displayed sender.

5. Three vulnerable forwarding features

  1. Open forwarding. Users can redirect to any destination with no recipient-side confirmation. 10 providers support this (incl. Outlook, Fastmail, iCloud, Hushmail, GoDaddy, Mail2World, Onet, Pobox, Runbox, Freemail).
  2. Relaxed validation for forwarded mail. Gmail, Outlook, Mail.ru accept forwarded mail even if SPF/DMARC fail, on the assumption that "a large forwarder must be doing the right thing".
  3. Unsolicited DKIM signatures for hosted domains. Shen 2021 already documented Yahoo/Office365/Alibaba; Liu 2023 finds three more — iCloud, Hushmail, Runbox — that sign the From header using the spoofed sender's domain (they manage that domain's DNS / DKIM keys). The signed results then pass DMARC alignment.

7. Disclosure & ethics

All attacks disclosed to affected providers. Outcomes:

  • Zoho: Patched ARC bug, awarded bug bounty.
  • Microsoft: Confirmed (severity "Important"), bounty, partial fix (only rejects REJECT-policy domains).
  • Gaggle: Promised to start enforcing DMARC.
  • Gmail: Fixed.
  • iCloud: Partial fix (still forwards DMARC-NONE failures).
  • Hushmail: Fixed (no longer forwards DMARC failures).
  • Freemail: Fixed (no longer spoofs domains it hosts).
  • Mail2World: Spam-filter work-around.
  • Runbox: No fix (treats as account abuse, not vulnerability).

Experiments were conducted only against accounts the authors controlled; spoofed "real" domain tests were validated with innocuous content and only against internal control addresses.

8. How MailSecLab uses this paper

  • I-series (forwarding / Received growth): The four forwarding mechanisms (PMF/MFEF/REM/REM+MOD) are the four semantic categories any synthetic hop-creation rule must respect. I1 (one hop) is PMF-like; I2 (chain forgery) is MFEF-like; I3 (multi-REM rewrite) is REM-like; Gaggle's flavor of I3 is the REM+MOD flavor.
  • L-series (multi-hop DKIM/ARC bearer): The §5.3 attack is exactly the K-series "ARC inspector"; L runs the chain across Linux + Postfix + OpenDKIM and other combinations of the 13 Linux/parser modules in MailSecLab.
  • Open-forwarding whitelist: The 10 providers supporting open forwarding (Outlook, iCloud, etc.) are the canonical targets for MailSecLab's I-series "ground-truth" experiments.
  • Domain taxonomy: The state.gov and transunion.com domains the paper lists as Attack-5.1-vulnerable become seed examples for H3 source-attribution experiments.
